About Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA)
PGA is the maximum expected ground acceleration at a site, expressed as a fraction of gravitational acceleration (g). It reflects a 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years (the MCEᵣ level used in building codes). National range and general hazard tiers:
PGA RangeGeneral Hazard LevelTypical Regions
< 0.05gVery LowCentral & eastern plains, much of the South
0.05 – 0.15gLowMidwest, mid-Atlantic, parts of the Southeast
0.15 – 0.30gModerateCentral US, Intermountain West
0.30 – 0.60gHighPacific Northwest, parts of Utah/Nevada
> 0.60gVery HighCoastal California, Cascadia subduction zone
These tiers are general context only and do not constitute a seismic design classification. Seismic Design Category (SDC) per ASCE 7 requires a licensed structural engineer. For interactive maps and full methodology, see https://earthquake.usgs.gov/hazards/.
Seismic Design Category (SDC) is an engineering determination per ASCE 7 and requires a licensed structural engineer. Hazard values are probabilistic estimates from the USGS national hazard model and do not constitute a site-specific geotechnical evaluation. Source: USGS NEHRP 2020 Seismic Hazard Maps.

About Wildfire Hazard Potential (WHP)
WHP is a continuous index produced by the USDA Forest Service Fire Modeling Institute. It reflects the relative potential for wildfire that would be difficult to control, based on fire weather, fuel conditions, and topography. It is not a probability of fire occurrence. The continuous index is typically grouped into five general classes:
WHP Range (approx.)Class
1 – 2,249Very Low
2,250 – 8,999Low
9,000 – 35,999Moderate
36,000 – 143,999High
≥ 144,000Very High
Class breakpoints are approximate and may vary by dataset edition. For full methodology and downloads, see https://www.firelab.org/project/wildfire-hazard-potential.
WHP is a modeled index, not a regulatory classification. Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) status and local fire hazard severity zones (FHSZ) may impose additional construction and insurance requirements. Consult your local fire authority and AHJ. Source: USDA Forest Service Fire Modeling Institute, {{ wildfire.vintage }}.

About the FEMA National Risk Index Score
The NRI composite score is a percentile-based value (0–100) representing a census tract's relative natural hazard risk compared to all other US tracts, incorporating expected annual loss, social vulnerability, and community resilience. FEMA uses five rating categories:
Score RangeFEMA Rating
0 – 20Very Low
20 – 40Relatively Low
40 – 60Relatively Moderate
60 – 80Relatively High
80 – 100Very High
Component hazard scores use the same 0–100 percentile scale relative to other US tracts. A higher score indicates higher relative risk for that hazard compared to the national distribution. For full methodology, data dictionary, and interactive map, see https://hazards.fema.gov/nri/.

Appendix

About This Report

What Plinth Is

Plinth is a site intelligence platform that aggregates publicly available federal and state datasets into a single, structured PDF report for a given US address. Reports are generated on demand and are intended to provide an organized starting point for predevelopment due diligence.

What This Report Is Not

This report is not an engineering study, Phase I or Phase II environmental site assessment, appraisal, wetlands delineation, title report, flood insurance determination, air quality permit review, geotechnical investigation, or legal opinion. Nothing in this report constitutes professional engineering, surveying, environmental, or legal advice. All interpretations, determinations, and recommendations regarding site suitability, development feasibility, risk management, or regulatory compliance require engagement of appropriately licensed professionals.

Data Accuracy & Limitations

All data values are sourced directly from the cited federal or state datasets and are presented without modification or interpretation. Known limitations of each dataset are described inline in the relevant report section. Dataset vintages vary; see Section 8 for effective dates and retrieval timestamps. Plinth does not verify real-time accuracy of source data.

No Risk Rating System

This report does not employ any color-coded risk rating, composite suitability score, or editorial judgment about site quality. All values are raw source data. FEMA National Risk Index scores are reproduced verbatim from FEMA's published model and are explicitly attributed to FEMA (see Section 2).

Regulatory Compliance Note

This report does not include individual-level consumer information and is not subject to the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). Reports are provided to business customers for commercial real estate due diligence purposes. Users are responsible for compliance with all applicable laws in their jurisdiction.

Report Retention

A snapshot of data source versions in effect at generation time is archived with this report's unique ID ({{ report_id }}). Archived reports represent conditions as of the report generation date and are not updated after delivery.
